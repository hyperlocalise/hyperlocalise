title: "Roles and permissions"
description: "Choose a workspace role for administration, development, translation, or review."

Your workspace role controls permitted actions. Team and project access determine where you can perform those actions. Organization-wide roles can have broader access; other members are limited to their permitted projects.

| Role | Intended responsibilities |
| --- | --- |
| Admin | Full workspace administration, including billing and organization settings. |
| Localization manager | Manage projects, connections, credentials, teams, and guidelines; approve and write back translations. |
| Developer | Work on projects and technical jobs, sync, and repositories; no translation approval or organization administration. |
| Reviewer | Work on jobs and AI actions; approve and write back translations; no organization administration. |
| Translator | Work on assigned jobs and draft translations; no approval or administration. |
| Member | Read permitted workspace, project, team, glossary, memory, and job information. |

Open **Members** → **Role permissions** for the permissions shown in your workspace. An administrator can update a member’s role.

## Choose roles for a translation team

Use translators for drafting and reviewers for final approval. Give localization managers responsibility for project setup and shared language resources. Reserve admin access for people who manage the workspace and billing.

## Vendors

Put an outside agency or freelancer in a **vendor team**: on **Teams**, select **Create team** and tick **Vendor team**. A freelancer is a vendor team of one. People in a vendor team keep a role such as **Translator** or **Reviewer**, and see their own work and nothing else in the workspace (the team's [manager](#a-vendor-teams-manager) also sees the tasks given to the team). Their role decides what they can do in that work, as it does for anyone in the editor.

A person in a vendor team is in no other team: adding someone to a vendor team removes them from their other teams. A vendor team holds no projects. The admin who makes a vendor team does not join it, and the workspace always keeps an admin outside vendor teams.

Their **My Jobs** lists the post-edit and proofread tasks given to them. From an open task they can:

- open the editor for the task's file, in the task's languages only
- save translations, comment, read the history of a string, and see screenshots already attached to it
- see the translation memory and glossary matches, and ask for an AI suggestion for a string, as their role allows
- download the file
- select **Mark done**

They work on text files. Images, video, office files and documents cannot be worked on through a task yet.

A task [waiting for another](/platform/translation/jobs#tasks-that-wait-for-another) is listed, and opens for work when the earlier one is finished. When a task is marked done, cancelled, or given to someone else, they can no longer open it, unless it is still open or waiting and given to a team they manage.

They cannot open projects, files, other jobs, the member list, teams, glossaries, memories, dictionaries, guidelines, project templates, Reports, issues, automations, the assistant, or settings. They cannot create API keys or use the API, MCP, the Slack and email assistants, or the design and content plugins, and cannot be the reviewer of a **Human approval** step.

Only post-edit and proofread tasks on a file can be given to them. Each person in a vendor team counts as a member of the workspace on your plan.

### A vendor team's manager

A task can also be given to the vendor team itself, so the agency decides who does it: choose the team in the task's owner picker. Give the agency's project manager the **Manager** role in the vendor team first: the picker lists only vendor teams with a manager who has joined the workspace and whose role can work on jobs. Their **My Jobs** lists every open or waiting task given to the team, and on a task's page they pick the person in the team who does it, or **Not handed out** to take it back. They can open the editor and select **Mark done** only on a task they gave to themselves. Everyone else in the team sees only the tasks handed to them.

The owner picker shows the team ticked while the task is with it. Choosing the team again takes the task back from the person it was handed to. Choosing another person or **Unassigned**, in the picker, the API or an automation, takes the task away from the team. If the team loses its manager, or its manager's role changes to **Member**, the tasks no one in the team has yet wait unseen and read as unassigned: give them to a person or to another team. Deleting the team leaves a task that was never handed out unassigned. The API, MCP and webhooks do not show the team: a task given to a team with no one handling it yet reads as unassigned there.

## A button is unavailable

Check the action’s role requirement, your team’s access to the project, and whether the resource belongs to an external TMS. Provider-owned resources can be read-only even when you can edit native resources.

Tokens inherit their owner’s current access and permitted scopes. Creating a token does not bypass role or project restrictions. See [Credentials](/platform/settings#api-keys) and [Access troubleshooting](/platform/help/access).
