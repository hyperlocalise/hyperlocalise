Most localisation tools stop at text.

They handle strings in your product, articles in your help centre and pages on your website. Then the campaign goes out, and the parts people actually see first are still in one language: the hero banner, the social ad, the launch video, the voiceover.

Those assets get localised by hand, if they get localised at all. A designer reopens the file for every market. An editor re-cuts the video to fit longer subtitles. An agency re-records the voiceover. Each market adds days, and the brand's approved terms get lost somewhere between the brief and the final export.

Today we're launching **[Iris](/iris)**, the AI localisation engine for multilingual assets. Iris creates and adapts images, video, voice and audio for every language and market, right from your Mac.

> **The idea is simple: every asset should feel local, not just the text around it.**

## What Iris does

Iris covers four jobs that used to need four different tools, and usually an agency.

| Capability | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Images, banners and social ads** | Rewrites the copy inside banners, social ads and product shots so each version reads as if it were made locally |
| **Video** | Subtitles and adapts product videos and video ads for each market without going back to the edit suite |
| **Voiceover and dubbing** | Dubs voiceovers in the speakers' own voices, or generates new voiceovers in the language of each audience |
| **Layout checks** | Catches cut-off text and broken layouts before a long German headline breaks your banner |

### Images that read as if they were made locally

Give Iris a finished image, such as a banner, a story or a product shot with copy baked in. It reads the text in the picture, translates it using your workspace's terminology, and redraws the approved copy in the same position, style and layout.

You don't need a layered design file or a clean background plate to start. The result sits next to the original as a new market version, so you can compare every locale side by side.

For a full walkthrough, read [How to Localise Image Ads and Social Creatives for Every Market](/blog/how-to-localise-image-ads-and-social-creatives-for-every-market).

### Video without re-editing

Iris translates a video's transcript for each market and times the subtitles like the original. It breaks lines where the speaker naturally pauses, not after a fixed number of words, and keeps names, numbers and units together.

When a translated line is too wide for the screen, Iris splits it into separate subtitles rather than shrinking the text until nobody can read it.

If you bring a finished video, such as an agency master or a social cut, Iris can rebuild it first. It reads the edit and the text burned into the frames, then rebuilds every on-screen word so it can be translated.

### Voices that match

Iris can dub speech in each market while keeping the speakers' own voices, and it can sync lip movement where someone speaks on camera. For a new voiceover, Iris translates the script and reads it in a voice suited to the market. If your brand has its own voice, such as a founder or a regular narrator, that voice can speak every language.

Brand and product names are a classic dubbing failure. Iris pronounces them the way your glossary spells them out, so you fix a mispronounced name once instead of in every script.

### Layout checks in every script

Translations change length. German, Finnish and French often run 20–35% longer than English. Chinese and Japanese run shorter. Arabic and Hebrew read right to left. Thai, Japanese and Chinese wrap lines without spaces between words.

Iris checks every market for text that overflows, captions that spill off screen, missing characters and fonts that don't cover the script. It shows you the frames with the most copy so a person can approve each locale before export.

## Built on the same brand rules as the rest of Hyperlocalise

Iris is not a standalone translation tool. It uses the same workspace as the [Hyperlocalise web app](/product/multilingual-content-studio):

- **Glossaries.** Approved translations are mandatory and forbidden ones are blocked everywhere: on-screen copy, voiceover scripts and image prompts.
- **Translation memory.** Copy you've already approved is reused instead of re-translated.
- **[Guidelines](/product/guidelines).** Copy can be checked against your uploaded brand and compliance documents.
- **Approvals feed memory.** When you approve a locale, the workspace remembers the approved copy for next time.

That's the difference between translating a creative and localising it. Fluent copy isn't enough if it uses the term legal banned last quarter. We've written about why [context matters more than fluency](/blog/ai-translation-is-not-enough-context-aware-localisation), and about [keeping brand voice and terminology consistent across markets](/blog/how-to-keep-brand-voice-and-terminology-consistent-across-markets). Iris applies the same rules to pictures and sound. See [Keeping Your Glossary and Brand Voice Consistent in Images and Video](/blog/keeping-glossary-and-brand-voice-consistent-in-images-and-video) for how.

## Market, not just language

Iris works with markets, not just languages. Spanish for Spain (`es-ES`) and Spanish for Mexico (`es-MX`) differ in vocabulary, tone and formatting. Prices, dates and plurals use each locale's own rules instead of being stitched together from translated fragments.

This is the core idea behind [hyperlocalisation](/blog/hyperlocalisation-why-global-growth-needs-more-than-translation): adapting communication so it feels natural and trusted in each market, not swapping words one for one.

## Works with your AI agents

Iris includes an MCP server, so agents like Claude Code can use its full toolset. That covers opening creatives, generating and localising images, subtitling and dubbing video, checking layouts, and reading your glossaries and translation memory.

You can ask your agent to "localise this campaign for France, Germany and Japan, using our glossary" and watch the work happen in the Iris app on your Mac. It's the same intelligence layer described in [What Is Translation Intelligence?](/blog/what-is-translation-intelligence), applied to creative assets. - **AI credit for generation.** Generating images, video and audio uses AI credit. Paid plans include monthly credit, starting at $20 a month. Iris runs image, video, voice and audio models from several AI vendors, and the [pricing page](/pricing) lists the rate for every model.
